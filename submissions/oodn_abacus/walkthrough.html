One Layer Deeper · a walkthrough

Teaching a network to square and wrap around

The task looks tiny: given three numbers, predict a fourth. Underneath sits an operation that clocks and cryptographers both lean on — and a machine that has to learn the arithmetic, not memorize the answers.

x^(2^T)  mod N
square, T times wrap mod N no lookup tables
1
The puzzle

Clock arithmetic, squared over and over

Everyone already does one kind of wrap-around math: a clock. Five hours after 10 o'clock isn't 15 — it's 3. You went past 12 and wrapped. That "wrap past N and take the remainder" is written mod N.

Now do just one move, over and over: take your number, square it, and wrap mod N. Then square that, wrap again. Do it T times. The dial above is exactly this — the amber marker lands on x, then x², then x⁴, each time bouncing to wherever it lands after wrapping around the ring of N positions.

Why anyone cares
Repeated squaring mod N is a time-lock. There's no known shortcut to the answer except to actually do the squarings one after another — which is why it's used to build puzzles that can only be opened after a set amount of computation. The whole task is: can a network shortcut what's designed to have no shortcut?
2
The one idea

It's a single operation, repeated — so learn the step

Look at the dial again: every hop is the same move — "square, then wrap." The answer at T=4 is just the answer at T=3 put through that move one more time.

That reframes everything. Instead of learning seven different problems (T = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64), the model learns one step — squaring-and-wrapping — and applies it again and again. Depth of computation is the clock: to go twice as far in time, loop the same little machine twice as many times. Get the one step exactly right and every depth comes for free.

learn 1 steploop it T times → any T generalizes
3
Why it's genuinely hard

To handle a new N, it must do the math — not recall it

Here's the twist that makes this a real research problem. The scoring rewards getting it right on moduli the model never trained on (call it "new N"). Two tempting shortcuts both collapse:

  • Memorize a table. Works for one fixed N, but a table for one modulus says nothing about a different one. Zero transfer to a new N.
  • Factor N and take a shortcut. For a small N you could crack it open (this is literally the RSA trapdoor). For a large secret N you can't — that impossibility is exactly what keeps RSA secure.

So the only route that scales is the honest one: learn the arithmetic of squaring-and-wrapping from the digits themselves, the way you'd learn long multiplication — a procedure that works on numbers it has never seen.

4
The model, in three parts

A small machine built to compute, not to remember

The network reads the three numbers as strings of digits and produces the answer's digits. Three design choices push it toward doing arithmetic. The pipeline:

Part A

Place value, not position

Each digit is tagged with which column it's in — units, tens, hundreds — instead of where it sits in the sentence. "The units digit" then looks the same whether the number has 3 digits or 6, so what it learns on small numbers still applies to bigger ones.

Part B

One block, looped

A single small block is run many times over. Reuse forces it to learn a reusable operation rather than memorize. Its attention can weigh all digits at once — the global "did we go past N?" check that wrapping needs.

Part C

Answer by column

The answer is read off one column at a time, aligned to the end — the same place-value idea in reverse — so it produces correct digits no matter how long the answer is.

Throughput

Enough practice reps

A subtle trap: with big batches the training data feeder stalled, so the model got only ~150 practice steps. A smaller batch gave ~10× more — a free fix that helps any model that can actually use the reps.

5
Where it stands — honestly

What already works, and the wall still ahead

Peeling the problem apart, the individual moves are learnable and they do generalize to unseen sizes:

add — 100% add-and-wrap — 100% wrap a given number — 97%

The stubborn part is multiplication with wrap — squaring a many-digit number. Chaining the learnable moves into it, with only the final answer as feedback, is where learning stalls. It's the same reason neural nets are famously shaky at exact long multiplication — and it's precisely the frontier this design is aimed at.

The honest bottom line
The competition collapses to one unsolved primitive — learning squaring-and-wrapping as a real procedure that transfers to new moduli. Loop that primitive and every time-depth follows automatically. This model is the best-known bet at that primitive; whether scale plus the right built-in bias is enough is exactly what the live runs are testing.
